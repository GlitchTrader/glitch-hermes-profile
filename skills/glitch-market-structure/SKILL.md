# Market Structure Vocabulary

This skill provides language for interpreting `market_structure_observations`.
It is consulted, not executed. Every concept is a hypothesis or measurement to
compare with current evidence; none is an entry rule, veto, sizing rule,
geometry template, or forecast.

## Auction-state hypotheses

- **Directional:** evidence may include sustained displacement, directional
  sequencing, changing participation, or acceptance away from a prior area.
- **Rotational:** evidence may include overlap, repeated two-sided response,
  unstable follow-through, or recurring boundaries.
- **Transition:** evidence may include failed continuation, changing volatility,
  disagreement across horizons, or insufficiently stable structure.

These labels can coexist or change quickly. The useful question is what each
label explains, what it fails to explain, and what current evidence would
falsify it.

## Acceptance, rejection, and liquidity events

A traded level can show continuation, rejection, temporary displacement,
liquidity consumption, or an unresolved test. Terms such as break, reclaim,
sweep, retest, and failure describe observed sequences; they do not prescribe
a response. Compare subsequent price behavior, participation, order flow,
volatility, and broader context before assigning meaning.

## Swing and sequence labels

Higher high, higher low, lower high, lower low, break of structure, and change
of character summarize selected pivots. Their meaning depends on pivot
selection, horizon, volatility, and follow-through. One label is never proof of
a regime change. `mixed` or unresolved structure is valid evidence rather than
a defect that must be forced into a directional story.

## Imbalance, reference levels, and retracement language

Imbalance, fair-value gap, session references, prior pivots, equal highs/lows,
retracement levels, and measured moves are candidate descriptions of where
market response may be informative. They are not inherently support,
resistance, targets, invalidation, or entry locations. Their value comes from
how current behavior confirms, contradicts, ignores, or reinterprets them.

## Volatility-normalized comparison

Use volatility and observed range to express distances and uncertainty in a
comparable way. Do not impose a fixed ATR threshold, point distance,
reward/risk ratio, or noise cutoff. The same absolute distance can represent
different information under different conditions, instruments, and horizons.

## Counterfactual discipline

For any structure-based interpretation, record at least one plausible competing
explanation and the evidence that would distinguish them. Recent outcomes and
prior attempts may reveal attribution or uncertainty, but they do not ban
re-entry, require reversal, or select inaction.

Pattern names and historical anecdotes may help retrieval or discussion, but no
unverified numeric base rate belongs in the decision authority. Current native
evidence and explicit uncertainty outrank vocabulary.
